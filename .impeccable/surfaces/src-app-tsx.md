Scope: the independent emergency-card demonstration SPA, including management and public emergency views. Mode: Operate. Audience: people with diabetes or caregivers exploring the public/private split, QR-code readers, and facilitators. Task: inspect synthetic visibility, simulate a predefined update and withdrawal, and observe comprehension without collecting identifiers. Success: the demo is unmistakable, the public/private boundary is understood at a glance, and the public view remains readable on mobile. Constraints: no real health data, clinical guidance, free text, persistence, real revocation, or changes to the existing site.

## Direction contract

THESIS: A familiar public-service information screen makes the public/private boundary obvious before any action; it refuses a data-dense medical form that could pass as operational.

OWN-WORLD: Brazilian urban-transit wayfinding lends the interface a deep-blue and teal shell, concise sans-serif labels, dense but orderly stop-like rows, and one explicit visibility route. Public and private states always have text labels and distinct markers; color never carries meaning alone. The light content surface and large controls remain readable in a bright community room or under uneven indoor lighting.

STORY: The facilitator opens the synthetic management view, the participant inspects labelled public and private blocks, selects a predefined contact update, compares the public preview, and simulates withdrawal. Every transition says that it exists only in this browser session.

FIRST VIEWPORT: On desktop, a persistent demonstration banner and standard top navigation sit above a title and a three-stop path labelled Ficha, Compartilhamento, and Prévia pública. The main work area shows the synthetic card with visibility labels; the side area contains predefined update/withdraw controls, and the facilitator checklist follows as a full-width section. On mobile, the path compresses to a short horizontal three-stop route; the card precedes controls and checklist, and the public preview remains one tap away.
